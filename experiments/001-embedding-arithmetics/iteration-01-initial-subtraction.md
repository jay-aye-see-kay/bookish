## 6. Assumptions proven or disproven

### Proven

- **Classic word analogies do hold in Qwen3-Embedding-8B.** `king - man + woman` ranks `queen` first with cosine 0.8478; `dog - puppy + kitten` ranks `cat` first at 0.8914. The model has at least some linear-compositional structure for single-token concepts.
- **Type markers steer the embedding.** Adding `Movie|`, `Game|`, or a sentence like `The 1995 film Hackers` consistently shifts nearest-book results toward the intended medium/theme and away from unrelated senses of the title.
- **Year markers cluster with each other.** `1995`, `1982`, and `2021` are mutually closer (0.73–0.77) than they are to media-type markers, so the model does represent years as a distinct semantic group.
- **Domain/type markers cluster loosely.** `Movie`, `Book`, and `Game` are all similar to each other (~0.62–0.64), and `Genre`/`Theme` form a tighter pair (0.7157). `Director` is more distinct.
- **The pipe `|` acts as a weak separator but not a magic one.** `Movie Hackers` (space) is closer to the natural sentence `The Movie Hackers` (0.9054) than `Movie|Hackers` is (0.8501). The pipe is not uniquely special; it is just one way to prepend a marker.

### Disproven

- **Vector subtraction does *not* strip domain markers to reveal a "purer" concept.** For every franchise tested, subtracting `Movie`/`1995` etc. produced a vector with strongly negative cosine similarities to relevant books and a top-neighbour list dominated by random, unrelated titles.
- **`Movie|1995|Hackers` is not a linear sum of `Movie`, `1995`, and `Hackers`.** If it were, subtraction would have recovered something close to `Hackers`. Instead the purified vector scored −0.2217 against *Neuromancer* and −0.2875 against *Snow Crash*, versus positive scores of 0.57–0.62 for the unprepared inputs.
- **Adding a year does not reliably improve over a simple type marker.** For Hackers, `Movie|1995|Hackers` was slightly worse than `Movie|Hackers` on *Snow Crash* (0.6061 vs 0.6054) and much worse than the sentence form. For Dune, `Movie|2021|Dune` was the worst of the Dune inputs (0.6805 to the known book vs 0.7192 for raw `Dune`).
- **Subtraction is not even benign; it is actively harmful.** The arbitrary control `Movie|1995|Hackers - Pineapple - Tuesday` still surfaces hacking/cyberpunk books, but with near-zero or negative scores, showing that subtraction mainly collapses/negates the signal rather than removing specific semantic components.

### Still unknown

- **Why analogies work but cross-modal subtraction fails.** Both operations are linear, so the model clearly supports *some* linear structure. We do not yet know what separates the analogy domain (single-token words with strong relational training) from the cross-modal domain (structured strings with medium/year markers).
- **Whether unnormalized subtraction would behave differently.** All vectors here were L2-normalized before and after subtraction. The magnitude of the pre-normalized difference might carry information, but cosine search discards it.
- **Whether a learned projection would succeed.** A fixed linear map trained on known (non-book input → book) pairs might do what naive subtraction cannot.
- **How well these findings generalise to obscure or ambiguous titles.** Every test case was a globally famous franchise. For an obscure film or a highly ambiguous title (e.g. `It`, `Cars`, `Up`), markers may matter more or less.
- **Whether `Movie Hackers` (space) outperforms `Movie|Hackers` (pipe).** The separator probe shows the space form is closer to natural-sentence embeddings, but we did not run nearest-book search on the space form.
- **Whether longer descriptive context (plot summary, director, cast) helps or hurts.** We only tested very short structured strings and one short sentence.

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Do not use vector subtraction to remove domain markers.** It degrades retrieval quality and can flip similarities negative.
2. **Use a natural sentence or a simple type prefix for non-book preferences.** For Hackers, `The 1995 film Hackers` gave the strongest thematic signal. For franchises where a sentence is awkward, `Movie|Title` or `Game|Title` is a pragmatic fallback.
3. **Do not add release years by default.** Years did not improve scores in these tests and sometimes hurt them.
4. **Keep the embedding cache keyed on the exact input string.** Since different formats produce meaningfully different vectors, we should store/cache embeddings for each format we actually use.

## 8. Follow-up experiments

1. **Separator comparison.** Run nearest-book search for `Movie Hackers`, `Movie: Hackers`, `Hackers (film)`, and `The 1995 film Hackers` side by side to decide the canonical input format.
2. **Obscure-title stress test.** Repeat the cross-media experiment with ambiguous or obscure titles (`It`, `Up`, `Cars`, `Contact`, `The Room`) to see whether type markers are necessary or sufficient.
3. **Projection learning.** Given pairs like `(Movie|Blade Runner, Book|Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?)`, fit a small linear projection matrix and test whether it maps cross-domain queries closer to their book counterparts than raw subtraction does.
4. **Unnormalized arithmetic.** Repeat the subtraction using raw (non-normalized) embeddings and compare L2 distance or use the raw dot product before re-normalising, to see if magnitude carries useful signal.
5. **Longer-context prompts.** Test inputs like `A 1995 cyberpunk film about teenage hackers: Hackers` or include a one-line plot summary, and measure whether richer context improves thematic alignment.
6. **Analogy boundary.** Systematically vary analogy complexity: single-token vs multi-token, concrete nouns vs abstract relations, common training examples vs rare ones, to map where linear composition holds.
7. **Year-marker interaction.** Test whether years help when the title alone is ambiguous (e.g. `Dune` 1984 vs 2021) or when the corpus contains multiple adaptations.
